What students already know, and the question they will investigate today.

You already know that the Sun rises in the east and sets in the west. You have seen the sky shift from soft morning light to bright midday, and then to warm evening colors. But why does the Sun seem to travel across the sky?

Core Learning Idea

Today we will learn that the Sun appears to move because Earth rotates from west to east.

How Earth's Rotation Creates Day and Night

💡

Think about it: If Earth turns, our view of the Sun changes even when the Sun stays in place.

My Notes

Action

A quiet, concrete model that lets students observe before naming the idea.

Use a flashlight as the Sun and a ball as Earth. Keep the light still while students slowly rotate the ball and observe how light reaches the marked place.

  1. Put a sticker on the ball to mark your place on Earth.
  2. Hold the flashlight completely still. It represents the Sun.
  3. Slowly rotate the ball from west to east.
  4. Observe how light falls on the sticker as the ball turns.

Experiment Setup

Observation

When does the sticker move into the light? When does it move into darkness? Ask students to record before discussing.

💡

Think about it: The flashlight stays still, so any change students see must come from the ball rotating.

My Notes

Analysis

Students sort money, read the printed value, and compare coins and notes that can have the same value.

Activity Discussion

When you sorted the coins and notes, how did you know which was which?

Coins are round and metal. Notes are flat and made of paper.

Visual · Sort Coins and Notes

Generalized Discussion

Can a coin ever have the same value as a note?

Yes. The ₹10 coin and the ₹10 note are both worth ₹10, even though one is metal and one is paper.

• A coin is round and metal; a note is flat and made of paper.
• The number printed on any coin or note tells us its value in rupees.
• Coins go up to ₹10; notes go from ₹10 to ₹100 and beyond.

Visual · Values Increase as Numbers Get Bigger

Higher-Order Thinking

Riya has a ₹10 coin and Dev has a ₹10 note. They both say they have ₹10. Who is right?

Both are right. A ₹10 coin and a ₹10 note have exactly the same value: ₹10. The form, coin or note, does not change the value.

Visual · Same Value, Different Forms

💡

Think about it: The material tells us whether money is a coin or a note, but the printed number tells us its value.

Coins are metal money. Notes are paper money. Students should name the type before comparing value.

Question 2 Compare

Which has a greater value: a ₹50 note or a ₹10 coin?

A₹50 note
B₹10 coin
CBoth are equal
DCannot compare
Show Answer Hide Answer
₹50 note

Compare the printed numbers, not the material. 50 is greater than 10, so the ₹50 note has greater value.

Differentiation

Those who need help

  • Let students handle real coins and notes to feel the difference, metal versus paper, before writing.
  • Provide a reference chart of coin and note images with values labelled for students to refer to.
  • For Q2, reduce to one pair at a time. Cover the second pair until the first is answered.
Differentiation

Those who need a challenge

Extension of Q3: After comparing the ₹50 note and ₹10 coin, ask: How many ₹10 coins would equal the same value as a ₹50 note? Write the number sentence. Answer: 5 × ₹10 = ₹50, so 5 coins.

💡

Think about it: Students should connect the material and printed value before comparing coins and notes.

Earth rotates on its axis from west to east. This makes the Sun appear to move across the sky from east to west each day.

BWest

East

The Sun rises in the east because Earth rotates toward the east. By noon it's high in the sky, and by evening it sets in the west.
