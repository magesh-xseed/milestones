Changes in the Sky Across the Day

Discover why the Sun appears to move and how Earth's rotation shapes morning, noon, evening, and night.

Lesson Introduction

What students already know, and the question they will investigate today.

You already know that the Sun rises in the east and sets in the west. You have seen the sky shift from soft morning light to bright midday, and then to warm evening colors. But why does the Sun seem to travel across the sky?

Core Learning Idea

Today we will learn that the Sun appears to move because Earth rotates from west to east.

Class essential

Action

A quiet, concrete model that lets students observe before naming the idea.

Use a flashlight as the Sun and a ball as Earth. Keep the light still while students slowly rotate the ball and observe how light reaches the marked place.

  1. Put a sticker on the ball to mark your place on Earth.
  2. Hold the flashlight completely still. It represents the Sun.
  3. Slowly rotate the ball from west to east.
  4. Observe how light falls on the sticker as the ball turns.

Experiment Setup

Observation

When does the sticker move into the light? When does it move into darkness? Ask students to record before discussing.

Analysis

Use a calm reading flow instead of nested boxes. Let the explanation unfold.

Question

What did you notice when Earth turned while the Sun stayed still?

Explanation

When Earth turned, the Sun appeared to move across the sky. The Sun did not move in the model. Our view changed because Earth rotated.

Think Deeper

If the Sun is low in the east, it is morning. If it is high overhead, it is noon. If it is low in the west, it is evening.

Visual · The Sun's Arc Across the Day

Application

Students apply the model, then extend it through home discussion.

Complete My Own Worksheet 1. Students use the Sun's position to explain time of day and connect the apparent movement of the Sun to Earth's spinning.

Differentiation · Need Help

Ask where the Sun is in the morning and evening if it is in the south at noon. Let the child point or act it out. Remind them that the Sun appears to move because Earth is spinning.

Differentiation · Need Challenge

The Sun is high in the sky but slightly toward the west. Ask what time of day it could be and have students explain the clue they used.

Lesson Summary

Today you discovered how the Sun appears to move across the sky.

It appears to move because Earth rotates. This rotation changes our view of the Sun through the day.

Key Takeaways
Morning

The Sun appears low in the east.

Noon

The Sun appears highest in the sky.

Evening

The Sun appears low in the west.
