Grade 2 · Science · Block 11 Light and Shadows

What Is This Block About?

Everything arjun will explore — explained the way a curious parent would want to read it.

This block helps arjun understand light, shadows, and why the sky changes from day to night.

1
They learn that light travels in straight lines and that shadows appear when light is blocked.
2
They observe how shadow size and direction change when the light source changes position.
3
They connect these ideas to the Sun, Earth’s rotation, and the pattern of day and night.

How Earth's Rotation Creates Day and Night

By the end of the 8 sessions, arjun should be able to explain everyday observations using simple science ideas instead of guesswork.

What Arjun Will Discover
  • Light travels in straight lines from its source — it can't bend around corners
  • Shadows form when an opaque object blocks the path of light
  • The size and direction of a shadow changes depending on where the light source is
  • The Sun is our primary natural light source and drives the cycle of day and night
  • Earth's rotation — not the Sun moving — explains why the sky changes throughout the day

The Big Ideas

Four foundational concepts arjun will build understanding around — each connected to the world they already know.

Concept 01
The Sun Only Appears to Move
  • The Sun does not move across the sky in the way it seems to.
  • The Earth spins from west to east.
  • That spin makes the Sun appear to rise in the east and set in the west.
Concept 02
Shadows Shift with the Sun
  • As the Sun's position changes, shadows change too.
  • Shadows are long in the morning and evening.
  • Shadows are shortest around midday and always fall on the side opposite the Sun.
Concept 03
Lower Light Makes Bigger Shadows
  • The closer the light source is, the bigger the shadow it casts.
  • The lower the light source is, the longer and larger the shadow appears.
Concept 04
Different Materials Cast Different Shadows
  • Opaque objects cast dark, sharp shadows.
  • Translucent objects cast faint shadows.
  • Transparent objects cast little or almost no shadow at all.

What Happens in Each Session

A walk through all 8 sessions — what arjun learns, what they do, and how long it takes.

1
Session 01
What is Light?
35 min Completed

Children explore the difference between natural light sources (the Sun, fire, stars) and artificial ones (bulbs, torches). They classify objects and discover that some things make their own light while others only reflect it.

Ask arjun today
Is the Moon a light source, or does it reflect light from the Sun?
What to observe at home
Walk through your home after dark and ask arjun to point to every light source they can find. Notice if they correctly spot that the TV reflects but doesn't make the same kind of light as a lamp.
2
Session 02
How Do Shadows Form?
35 min Completed

Using torches and simple objects, children discover that shadows only form when light is blocked. They observe that the shadow always appears on the opposite side of the object from the light source.

Ask arjun today
If I shine a torch at you from your left side, which side will your shadow appear on?
Real-world connection
Next time you're outside, ask arjun to predict which direction their shadow will fall before they check. Small conversations like this build deep scientific intuition.
3
Session 03
Shadow Shapes and Sizes
35 min Completed

Children experiment with changing shadow size by moving the torch nearer and farther from the object. They record observations and begin to see the pattern: closer light = bigger shadow, farther light = smaller shadow.

Ask arjun today
Why do you think your shadow gets longer or shorter when the light moves?
Try this at home
Use a torch and a small toy in a dim room. Move the torch closer and farther away, and ask arjun to predict how the shadow will change before they see it.
4
Session 04 — Current
Light Travels in Straight Lines
35 min In Progress

A pivotal session where children discover why light can't curve around obstacles. Using card with holes in a straight line, they show that light only passes through when all holes are perfectly aligned — proving that light travels in straight lines.

Ask arjun today
If I put a wall between us in a dark room and shone a torch at the wall, would any light reach you? Why not?
Real-world connection
Fibre optic cables actually do "bend" light — but using tiny mirrors, not by bending the light itself. If arjun is curious about this, that's a sign of exceptional thinking.
5
Session 05
Transparent, Translucent and Opaque
35 min Upcoming

Children test everyday materials — glass, tracing paper, cardboard, plastic — to classify how much light passes through each. They learn three new vocabulary words that describe how materials interact with light, and connect this to why windows are made of glass.

6
Session 06
The Sun: Our Star
35 min Upcoming

Zooming out to the bigger picture: the Sun is introduced as a star — a natural light source 150 million km away. Children learn how the Sun's position in the sky changes throughout the day, and explore why we should never look directly at it.

7
Session 07
Day and Night
35 min Upcoming

The Earth-rotation model is introduced. Using a globe and a torch, children model how the Earth spinning on its axis causes one side to face the Sun (day) while the other faces away (night). This is often a genuine "aha moment" for 7-year-olds.

8
Session 08
The Earth–Sun Relationship
35 min Upcoming

The block's capstone session. Children consolidate their understanding of Earth's orbit around the Sun, connecting it to seasons, the length of days, and patterns they've observed in everyday life. They apply all five big ideas to explain a set of real-world scenarios.

Questions to Ask Arjun

Great science conversations start with curiosity. These prompts are designed for the dinner table, the car, or a morning walk.

This question directly checks whether arjun understands the relationship between the Sun's position and shadow length — one of the core ideas in Session 3 and 6. In the morning, the Sun is low on the horizon, so its light hits at a steep angle, creating long shadows. By noon, the Sun is almost overhead, making shadows nearly disappear.

💡 Tip: Step outside in the morning and again at noon to test it together!

This tests whether arjun can apply the shadow-size rule in reverse — and think about it from the object's perspective rather than the light source's. Moving closer to the source makes the shadow grow larger behind you. Moving away makes it shrink. Many children intuitively think the opposite.

💡 Tip: Try this with a torch in a dark room — the surprise usually makes it stick!

This is the big conceptual shift of Session 7. It looks like the Sun moves across the sky — but that's an illusion created by Earth's rotation. Arjun should be able to explain that the Earth spins, not the Sun, and that's why different parts of the world are in daylight at different times.

💡 Tip: Use a ball and a torch in a dark room to model it. Let them spin the "Earth" themselves!

From Session 1, this tests the distinction between luminous objects (Sun, candle, bulb, fire) and non-luminous objects (Moon, mirror, paper). The Moon is the classic trick question — it looks like a light source but only reflects sunlight. If arjun gets that one right, they've really understood the concept.

💡 Tip: Look around whatever room you're in — make it a competition to spot more objects in each category!

This draws on Session 5's vocabulary: transparent, translucent, and opaque. Glass is transparent (light passes through clearly), frosted glass is translucent (light passes through but gets scattered), and a wall is opaque (no light passes through at all). If arjun can use these three words correctly, that's excellent science vocabulary for Grade 2.

💡 Tip: Test different materials at home — cling film, paper, thick cloth, a glass of water — and sort them together.

Try It at Home

Two parent-and-child activities that bring the classroom science to life. No special equipment needed.

Activity 01
Connected to Session 2
15–20 mins Easy Session 2 — Shadow Tracing
Shadow Tracing

Trace the same object’s shadow at different times of the day to help arjun see how sunlight changes shadow length and direction.

Steps
  1. 1
    Choose a sunny spot and tape the paper down.
  2. 2
    Place the object on the paper and trace its shadow in the morning.
  3. 3
    Trace it again at noon and once more in the late afternoon.
  4. 4
    Compare the three outlines and ask which one is longest and why.

Why This Matters: The changing outlines make sunlight feel observable, not theoretical, so the pattern becomes easier to notice and explain.

You'll Need
Large sheet of paper Pencil or chalk Small toy or object Tape Sunny day
Parent Tip

Write the time beside each tracing and ask arjun to notice both the size and the direction of the shadow.

Activity 02
Connected to Session 7
20–30 mins Easy Session 7 — Day & Night
Make an Earth–Sun Model

Use a torch and a ball to model how Earth’s rotation causes day and night. Arjun can see which side faces the light and which side stays dark.

Steps
  1. 1
    Mark a dot on the ball to show “your city.”
  2. 2
    Switch off the lights and hold the torch steady as the Sun.
  3. 3
    Let arjun slowly spin the ball and identify when your city is in day or night.
  4. 4
    Tilt the ball slightly and repeat to show how changing angles affect daylight.

Why This Matters: It turns an abstract astronomy idea into something arjun can hold, rotate, and remember with confidence.

You'll Need
Torch or lamp Any round ball Marker pen Dark room Small sticker
Parent Tip

Keep the torch still and let arjun do the turning. Ask, “Is our city in day or night now?” and let them explain before you help.
