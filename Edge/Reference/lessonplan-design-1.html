Lesson 1 · 35 minutes

Changes in the Sky Across the Day

Aim

Explain why the Sun appears to move in the sky and how it affects a day.

We already know the sun rises in the East and sets in the west. Today, we will learn why the sun appears to move in the sky and how this affects the activities we do in a day.

New Words

appears looks like; seems to be
rotate to turn or move around a centre point

Resources

  • •Flashlight (Sun)
  • •Ball (Earth) with circle sticker — 1 set per group of 5
  • •My Own Worksheet 1, Notebook, Mastery Book
family_restroom

Parent Tips

Tip 1: Explain that the Sun appears to move because the Earth spins from west to east. It's like when you are moving on a train, and the trees seem to move in the opposite direction.

Tip 2: As the child turns the ball, help them notice that the Sun seems to "appear" on one side and "disappear" on the other—even though the Sun never moved. This makes the idea practical: the Earth's spin makes the Sun look like it rises and sets.

ACTION · Group Work · 15 minutes

Put a sticker on the ball to show your place on Earth.

Keep the flashlight still.

Rotate the ball slowly from west to east.

Observe how light falls on the sticker.

Think:

As you move the ball slowly, what happens to the sticker as it moves between light and darkness? How does this affect the time of day?

ANALYSIS · 10 minutes

Activity Discussion

What did you notice when the Earth (ball) turned around the Sun (flashlight)?

Explanation: When the Earth turned around, the Sun appeared to move even though it was still.

Generalized Discussion

Why does the Sun appear to move in the sky, and how does that change the day?

Explanation: The Sun appears to move because the Earth rotates from west to east. This spinning makes the Sun seem to rise in the east, stay high above our head at noon, and set in the west.

Higher Order Thinking

If the Sun is low in the east, what time is it? If it is high in front of you, what time is it? If it is low in the west, what time is it?

Explanation: Low in the east → morning. High in front → noon. Low in the west → evening.

APPLICATION

Complete My Own Worksheet 1

Needs Help

Ask: where is the Sun in the morning (east) and evening (west) if it is in the south at noon? Encourage the child to point or act it out.

Challenge

The Sun is seen high in the sky but slightly towards the west. What time of day could it be? Explain how you know.

HOMEWORK

Answer Quick Quiz 1–4 in the Mastery Book.

Parent Tip Q3: Encourage your child to act it out — stand in one place as the Sun and slowly spin like the Earth.

Parent Tip Q4: Prompt your child to look for more than one clue. Ask: Where do you usually see the Sun in the morning? Is the sky always bright only in the morning?

ASSESSMENT

A

Approaching

Notice that the Sun appears to move in the sky.

M

Meeting

Explain why the Sun appears to move in the sky and how it affects a day.

E

Exceeding

Predict different times of the day based on the position of the sun in the sky.

Lesson 2 · 35 minutes

How Shadows Change During the Day

Aim

Explain how the position of the Sun in the sky affects shadows.

We already know the sun appears to move in the sky. Today, we will learn how the position of the sun affects shadow formation.

New Words

shadowa dark shape made when an object blocks light
anglesthe space between two lines that meet at a point, measured in degrees
axisan invisible line on which something turns or rotates

Resources

  • •Flashlight
  • •An object to cast shadows
  • •Labelled cardinal directions (1 set per group)
  • •My Own Worksheet 2, Notebook, Mastery Book
family_restroom

Parent Tips

Tip 1: Let your child use a torch and any object while they read the text to cast shadows. Gently guide them to notice: The shadow is always on the opposite side of the light source.

Tip 2: Help your child relate the Sun's position in the sky to the shadow size. Ask them: What might change in the shadow formation if the Sun rises in the west instead of the East?

ACTION · Group Work

Place the object on a table. Use the flashlight as the Sun and hold it in the direction of East.

Switch on the light so it falls on the object and casts its shadow.

Slowly move the flashlight so it is directly above the object.

Lastly, position the flashlight in the West direction.

Observe and note the size and direction of the shadow for every position in your notebook.

Sun's Position Size of Shadow Position of Shadow
Eastobservation hereobservation here
Directly aboveobservation hereobservation here
Westobservation hereobservation here

ANALYSIS

Activity Discussion

What did you notice about the shadows when the flashlight changed its position, and how are shadows formed?

Explanation: When we moved the flashlight, the shadow's size and position changed. Shadows are formed when an object blocks light.

Generalized Discussion

How does the Sun's position in the sky affect the shadows that are formed?

Explanation: As the Sun's position changes in the sky, the angle at which sunlight falls on objects also changes. Based on this, the shadows also change in size and position.

Higher Order Thinking

What time of the day will it be if your shadow is long and pointing to the East?

Explanation: If your shadow is longer and pointing to the East, it is evening — shadows are cast in the direction opposite to the source of light.

APPLICATION

Complete My Own Worksheet 2

Needs Help

Give a hint: The Sun is on the opposite side of the shadow. Ask: Which way is the shadow pointing? So where should the Sun be?

Challenge

Shadow A is very long and points west. Shadow B is very short. Shadow C is long and points east. Predict the time of day for A, B, and C.

HOMEWORK

Answer Quick Quiz 1–4 in the Mastery Book.

Parent Tip Q1: Ask your child to try a quick experiment with a torch and an object at home. Ask: Can a shadow form without light?

Parent Tip Q2: Ask your child to explain how they know if the statement is true. What did they observe that helped them decide?

ASSESSMENT

A

Approaching

Observe how shadows form.

M

Meeting

Explain how the position of the Sun in the sky affects shadows.

E

Exceeding

Predict different times of the day based on the features of shadows and vice versa.

Lesson 3 · 35 minutes

Light and Shadow Relationship

Aim

Explain how shadows are formed and how their size and length change depending on the distance and angle of light.

New Words

surfacethe top or outside part of an object
distancehow far one place or object is from another

Resources

  • •A torch
  • •An object (pencil)
  • •A screen (plain wall or white sheet) — 1 set per group
  • •My Own Worksheet 3, Notebook, Mastery Book

ACTION · Group Work · 15 minutes

Light travels in straight lines

Shine a torch on a pencil. Place the pencil horizontally, then vertically. Notice how the light travels and the shape of the shadow.

Distance affects shadow size

Bring the torch closer to the object — notice the shadow size.

Take the torch away from the object — notice the shadow size.

Angle affects shadow size

Keep the torch at a low angle — observe shadow length.

Keep the torch at a high angle — observe shadow length.

Torch Distance Shadow Size Torch Position Shadow Length
Torch closerSmaller / BiggerLow angleShorter / Longer
Torch fartherSmaller / BiggerHigh angleShorter / Longer

ANALYSIS

Activity Discussion

How are shadows formed, and what are the conditions necessary to form shadows?

Explanation: A shadow forms when an object blocks light from reaching a surface. For a shadow to form, an object must be placed between a light source and a screen.

Generalized Discussion

Which shadow was longest? Which was shortest? Why do distance and angle affect shadow length?

Explanation: Shadows get bigger when the light is closer (~30°), smaller when farther (~90°). Changing angles makes shadows longer or shorter.

Higher Order Thinking

Predict how shadows will change if we move the flashlight closer or farther from the object at a 10° angle.

Explanation: If the flashlight is closer, the shadow will become bigger and longer. If farther, smaller and shorter — even at a 10° angle.

APPLICATION & HOMEWORK

Complete My Own Worksheet 3

Homework: Answer Quick Quiz 1–4 in the Mastery Book.

Parent Tip Q3: Help your child connect to real life by observing shadows outdoors at morning, noon, and evening.

Parent Tip Q4: Encourage tracing the path of light with their finger from each torch to the screen.

ASSESSMENT

A

Approaching

Notice that shadows form when an object blocks light.

M

Meeting

Explain how the distance and angle of light affect shadow size and length.

E

Exceeding

Predict shadow changes for different light distances and angles, and record observations accurately.

How Materials Affect Shadows

Aim

Explain how different materials affect shadow clarity.

We already know shadows can change size and shape. Today, we will learn how different materials affect shadow formation.

New Words

opaquedoes not let light pass through
transparentlets light pass through completely
translucentlets some light pass through
sharpclear shadow with well-defined edges
blurryunclear, fuzzy, not well-defined

Resources

  • •Torch & black chart paper (screen)
  • •Clear glass jar, butter paper, aluminium sheet
  • •Notebook, cling film, eyeglasses
  • •My Own Worksheet 4, Notebook, Mastery Book

ACTION

Use a flashlight and black chart paper to cast shadows of the given objects. Record observations in the table.

Object Light passes fully Light passes faintly No light passes
Clear glass jar
Butter paper
Aluminium sheet
Notebook
Cling film
Eyeglasses

ANALYSIS

Activity Discussion

How are shadows cast by transparent, translucent, and opaque objects different from each other?

Opaque → solid, dark shadow. Translucent → soft, less solid shadow. Transparent → faint or no clear shadow.

Generalized Discussion

What did the light spot on the wall tell you about the material?

Bright spot → transparent. Faint spot → translucent. No spot → opaque. Shadow clarity connects directly to light strength.

Higher Order Thinking

Predict what shadow will cast when light is passed through a tinted plastic raincoat.

The shadow will be faint and slightly colored. The tinted plastic is partially transparent — some light passes, the tint adds colour.

APPLICATION & HOMEWORK

Complete My Own Worksheet 4

Homework: Answer Quick Quiz 1–4 in the Mastery Book.

Parent Tip Q3: Show a book and a plastic bottle. Ask which one blocks more light.

Parent Tip Q4: Help your child recall the 3 things needed for a shadow: light source, object, and surface.

Needs Help

Ask: Which one is clear? Which shadow looks darkest? Which looks faint?

Challenge

Predict shadows for: a thin white curtain, a colored plastic folder, and a metal plate. Explain using material type.

ASSESSMENT

A

Approaching

Notice that some materials make dark shadows and others do not.

M

Meeting

Explain how opaque, translucent, and transparent materials affect shadow clarity.

E

Exceeding

Predict the shadow appearance for new materials and describe why based on light passing through.

ANALYSIS

Activity Discussion

What do you notice about the Sun's position in the sky during the day?

Explanation: Low in the east in the morning. Higher in the sky later. Low in the west in the evening.

Generalized Discussion

How does the Sun appear to move because of Earth's rotation, and how does it affect time of day?

Explanation: As the Earth rotates from west to east, the Sun appears to rise in the east and move to the west — morning → noon → evening.

HOTS Discussion

Japan is in the East; the USA is in the West. How differently will these countries experience different times of the day?

Explanation: Japan has morning first. While it is morning in the USA, Japan would already have noon.
