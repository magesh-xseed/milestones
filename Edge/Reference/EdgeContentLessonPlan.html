Why does the Sun appear to move across the sky?

A concise, teacher-focused lesson plan to demonstrate Earth's rotation and guide student observations.

TEACHER SUMMARY

A short, strong summary for quick scanning: Demonstrate Earth's rotation using a student volunteer, a flashlight (Sun), and a labelled Earth printout. Emphasize East/West and the apparent motion of the Sun.

AIM

8 mins

We will observe how Earth's rotation makes the Sun appear to move and link student observations to cardinal directions.

Key Steps
  1. Tape Earth printout to a volunteer.
  2. Fix flashlight on a chair as the Sun.
  3. Have volunteer slowly rotate to show the apparent Sun motion.

ANALYSIS

10 mins
Activity Discussion

Ask 3 students what they noticed and record common observations: Sun appears to move, direction cues, time-of-day predictions.

Sun PositionPart of Day
Coming from left (East)Morning
Directly in frontNoon
Moving to right (West)Evening
Higher-order Thinking

Ask students to predict what would happen if Earth's rotation speed changed or direction reversed.

APPLICATION

17 mins
Classwork · Worksheet 1

Students label times of day based on Sun position. Provide scaffold for those who need help.

Teacher Differentiation
  • Remind students which side is East/West.
  • Offer extra challenge: change rotation direction hypothetically.

Teacher Reference

Earth rotates from west to east, causing the apparent east→west motion of the Sun. Use this reference when clarifying misconceptions.
