Why does the Sun appear to move across the sky?

A high-fidelity, teacher-focused module demonstrating Earth's rotation and correlating observations to astronomical principles.

A clear demonstration is key: Use a student (Earth), a flashlight (Sun), and a labeled printout to show that the Sun's apparent motion is caused by Earth's West-to-East rotation.

lightbulbAIM & KEYWORDS

schedule8 mins

Students will observe how Earth's rotation causes the Sun's apparent movement and correctly associate this motion with cardinal directions (East & West).

buildSetup Steps
  1. Secure Earth printout to student (ensure correct poles/locations).
  2. Place flashlight (Sun) on a fixed elevated surface.
  3. Review West → East rotation direction before starting.

schedule8 mins
1
Initialize & Align
Designate classroom East and West clearly. Position volunteer so 'Sun' is at 'morning' (East).
2
Execute Rotation
Volunteer performs a slow, controlled West → East rotation. Students track the Sun's apparent East → West motion.
lightbulb_outlineTeacher Strategy
Have students focus on a specific point on the Earth printout (like their city) to truly grasp the concept of local day and night.
expand_moreView Detailed Script & Misconception Check
  1. Scripting: "Notice that you are spinning, not the Sun. This spin is called rotation."
  2. Observation Cue: Ask students: "When the volunteer faces the Sun, what time of day is it for them?"
  3. Misconception: Address the common belief that the Sun orbits the Earth.

psychologyHigher-Order Thinking (HOTS)

Challenge students to apply the rotation concept to hypothetical scenarios, promoting deeper conceptual understanding.

Prompt 1: If Earth rotated twice as fast, how long would a 'day' be?
Prompt 2: If Earth rotated East → West, where would the Sun appear to rise?
