AIM

We already know that fat is a very important nutrient required by the body because it provides energy. Today, we will learn to differentiate between different types of fats.

Teacher Tip

Tell students that fats are present in foods that leave a translucent patch. Prepare a set of filter papers and dry them beforehand. Show them to the students. Invite a few students to fill the table on the board.

ANALYSIS

10 mins
PART 1

ACTIVITY DISCUSSION

OK, now let's discuss the activity.

Question: How can you identify the presence of fats in a given food item?

Let's hear from at least 3 students!

ACTIVITY EXPLANATION
  • Foods that contain fats leave a translucent spot on filter paper.
  • Foods like vegetable oil, butter, and peanuts have fats in them.
  • Foods like fruit juice do not contain fats. They may wet the filter paper which dries up after sometime.
Food Items Translucent spot seen on filter paper (Yes/No) Fats present/absent
Vegetable oil Yes Present
Butter Yes Present
Peanuts Yes Present
Juice No Absent
PART 2

GENERALIZED DISCUSSION

OK, now let's generalize our discussion.

Question: Which food items in the demonstration are saturated and unsaturated fats and how are they different?

Let's hear from at least 3 students!

GENERAL EXPLANATION
  • Butter is an animal fat. Animal fats, like those present in red meat, are mostly saturated fats.
  • Saturated fats tend to exist in the solid form at room temperature.
  • Vegetable oil and peanuts are obtained from plants. Fats from plants and few animals, like fish, are unsaturated fats.
  • Most unsaturated fats exist in the liquid form at room temperature.

Saturated vs. Unsaturated Fats

Saturated Fats
Mostly present in animal foods like red meat
Should be eaten in limited amounts
Unsaturated Fats
Mostly present in plant-based foods and in fish
Can be eaten more than saturated fats
PART 3

HIGHER-ORDER THINKING

OK, now let's try some higher-order thinking!

Question: Consuming too much fatty foods can lead to various diseases. Are all fats bad for us? Why/Why not?

Let's hear from at least 3 students!

HOTS EXPLANATION
  • While too much fat can be harmful to our body, fat is an essential nutrient that protects organs, provides energy when needed, and regulates body temperature.
  • Studies have shown that unsaturated fats are healthier than saturated fats.
  • Saturated fats increase blood cholesterol level which, in turn, increases the risk of heart diseases and can even lead to heart failure.
  • Unsaturated fats regulate the blood cholesterol level and are good for our heart and our overall health.

Teacher Tip

If students can understand, discuss about HDL (good) and LDL (bad) cholesterol types in simple terms to help them understand that all types of cholesterol are not necessarily bad for the body.
