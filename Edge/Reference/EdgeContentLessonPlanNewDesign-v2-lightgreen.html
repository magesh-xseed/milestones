Why does the Sun appear to move across the sky?

AIM & KEYWORDS

8 mins

Students will observe how Earth's rotation causes the Sun's apparent movement and correctly associate this motion with cardinal directions (East & West).

Setup Steps
  1. Secure Earth printout to student (ensure correct poles/locations).
  2. Place flashlight (Sun) on a fixed elevated surface.
  3. Review West → East rotation direction before starting.

ANALYSIS

10 mins
PART 1: ACTIVITY DISCUSSION
Question: What did you notice when the Earth (teacher/student) turned around the Sun (flashlight)?
Let's hear from at least 3 students!

Activity Explanation

  • When the Earth turned around, the Sun appeared to move across the room, even though the flashlight was still.
  • This apparent movement is an illusion caused by the Earth moving.

Recorded Observations

Sun Position (from the observer on Earth) Part of the Day
The Sun is coming in from your left side (East)Morning
The Sun is directly in front of you (High)Noon
The Sun is moving toward your right side (West)Evening
PART 2: GENERALIZED DISCUSSION
Question: Why does the Sun appear to move in the sky, and how does that change the day?
Let's hear from at least 3 students!

General Explanation

  • The Sun appears to move because the Earth rotates (spins) on its axis.
  • The Earth rotates from **West to East** (counter-clockwise when viewed from the North Pole).
  • This spinning causes the Sun to appear to rise in the East, be high above our head at noon, and set in the West, completing one day.

PART 3: HIGHER-ORDER THINKING
Question: If the Sun is low in the east, what time is it? If it is high in front of you, what time is it? If it is low in the west, what time is it?
Let's hear from at least 3 students!

HOTS Explanation / Prediction

  • If the Sun looks low in the east, it is **morning** (sunrise).
  • If it is high right in front of you (or to the South in the Northern Hemisphere), it is **noon** (midday).
  • If the Sun is low in the west, it is **evening** (sunset).

APPLICATION & WORK

17 mins
CLASSWORK · WORKBOOK (10 MINS)

Students apply their learning by labeling key times of day and directions on an Earth rotation diagram.

description Worksheet 1.1: Rotation Diagram
Teacher Differentiation Tips

This is a good time to walk around the class and check on student understanding, especially their grasp of East/West movement.

Those needing more help (A)

Provide a pre-labeled compass rose on the student's desk and remind them which side was East (the left side when facing the "Sun" flashlight) and which side was West (the right side).

Extra Challenge (E)

Assign an optional research prompt on the concept of the celestial sphere (the apparent sphere of the sky).

CLASSWORK · EXTENDED SCENARIO (7 MINS)

Students write a short, 3-sentence description of what they would observe if they lived on the opposite side of the world, linking it to the concept of rotation.

auto_storiesWrap-up Creative Task
Have students create a 3-panel comic strip demonstrating Earth's rotation causing day and night.
HOMEWORK

Observe the Sun's position from your home at three different times tomorrow (morning, noon, evening) and record the cardinal direction.
