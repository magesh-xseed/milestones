Parent Tips

Tip 1: Explain that the Sun appears to move because Earth spins from west to east — just like how trees seem to rush backward when you're on a moving train.

Tip 2: As your child turns the ball, guide them to see the Sun "appear" on one side and "disappear" on the other — even though the Sun never moved.

Action Group Work · 15 mins

Hands-on experiment — do it, observe, and record

Use a flashlight as the Sun and a ball as the Earth to see how Earth's rotation makes the Sun appear to move.

Step-by-Step

1
Put a sticker on the ball to mark your place on Earth.
2
Hold the flashlight completely still — it is the Sun and never moves.
3
Slowly rotate the ball from West to East.
4
Observe how light falls on the sticker as the ball turns.

Experiment Setup

Observation Prompt

As you rotate the ball — when does the sticker move into the light? When does it go dark? Record your observations in your notebook.

psychology

Think About This

As you move the ball slowly, what happens to the sticker as it moves between light and darkness? How does this represent the change in time of day?

Analysis 10 minutes

Discuss, explain, and deepen your understanding

Activity Discussion

What did you notice when the Earth (ball) turned around the Sun (flashlight)?

check_circle When the Earth turned, the Sun appeared to move — even though it was completely still.

Explanation: Earth's rotation makes the Sun appear to travel across our sky. The Sun itself stays in one place — it is our perspective that changes as Earth spins.

Generalized Discussion

Why does the Sun appear to move in the sky, and how does that change the day?

info The Sun appears to move because Earth rotates from west to east. This makes the Sun seem to rise in the east, stay high at noon, and set in the west.

Higher Order Thinking

If the Sun is low in the east, what time is it? High in front? Low in the west?

lightbulb Low in east → morning. High overhead → noon. Low in west → evening.

Lesson Summary

Changes in the Sky Across the Day

1

The Sun does not move — the Earth rotates from west to east.

2

Earth's rotation makes the Sun appear to rise in the east and set in the west.

3

Sun's position (low east → high south → low west) tells us the time of day.

key

Key Takeaway

"The Sun appears to move because we are the ones moving — Earth's spin changes our view of the Sun all day long."
